We evaluate a small, detachable causal finite-impulse-response (FIR) residual for sequential recommendation on Amazon Reviews 2023 (5-core, full-catalog, leave-last-out). Matched-initialization internal estimates are positive on Musical\_Instruments (+0.002265, ordinary Welch 95\% CI [0.001928, 0.002602]) and, under one untuned configuration, on Industrial\_and\_Scientific (+0.002110 [0.001820, 0.002399]) and CDs\_and\_Vinyl (+0.006150 [0.005849, 0.006450]); the latter two use paired configuration blocks. These are outcome-known or test-exposed filter-versus-identity studies, not independent confirmation. A pre-declared but likewise outcome-known six-arm control study found that learned taps did not separate from a shared causal filter (-0.000081 [-0.000337, 0.000175]) or parameter-matched nonlinear causal control (+0.000204 [-0.000038, 0.000446]). A subsequent parameter-matched current-position-only placebo changed identity by -0.000069 [-0.000200, +0.000061], whereas learned FIR exceeded that placebo by +0.001941 [+0.001788, +0.002095] in the frozen three-test Holm family. This supports temporal access relative to that non-temporal placebo in this outcome-known setting, not per-channel-tap necessity, independent confirmation, or generalization. Secondary findings are two environment-caveated per-category point-estimate threshold comparisons and a Musical\_Instruments frequency-5-heavy text-tail effect; neither licenses a general system-ranking or cross-dataset claim.
