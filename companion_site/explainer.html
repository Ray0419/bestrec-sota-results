Section 1

The task: guess the next purchase — from the whole catalog

Someone bought guitar strings in January, a capo in March, a clip-on tuner last week. What do they buy next? That's the entire game, called sequential recommendation. Our version is deliberately the hard, honest variant:

  • Open exam, not multiple choice. Many papers hide the right answer among 99 decoys. We make the model rank every product in the catalog — 25,612 items for Video Games, 77,551 for Office Products — and check where the true next purchase landed.
  • The answers are hidden from training. Each person's final purchase is held out; the model is graded on predicting what it never saw.

How scoring works (NDCG@10): think of the model's guess as a search-results page. If the true next purchase shows up in the top 10, the model earns credit — more the higher it ranks. On an open exam over tens of thousands of items, good scores look small: 0.03–0.07 is the competitive range, and third-decimal differences matter the way tenths of a second matter in a sprint.

Section 2

How the model thinks — try being the recommender

The model reads a purchase history like a sentence: attention is a learned highlighter (which past items matter for this guess?), time bias makes fresher clues count more, and every product carries a "scent" — a numeric fingerprint of its title and description, so items that read alike smell alike, even if they were rarely purchased.

    Section 3 · the novel component

    The causal FIR filter — a shock absorber for taste

    Our main modeling contribution is a tiny idea borrowed from signal processing. The signal flowing through the model — "what is this person into right now?" — is noisy: one impulse buy (a gag gift, a purchase for a friend) jolts it like a pothole. We bolt on an FIR filter: engineering-speak for a short, learned weighted average that smooths each moment using only the few moments before it.

    • A shock absorber: bumps still register; they stop rattling the frame.
    • A coffee filter: flavor through, grounds held back.
    • A moving average on a stock chart: wiggle becomes trend.

    Three properties keep it honest: it is causal (only ever looks backward — a forecaster allowed yesterday's data, never tomorrow's; even subtle future-peeking is a classic way papers fool themselves), it starts switched off (zero-initialized — it must earn influence during training), and it is tiny (a knob, not a new engine).

    Section 4 · the finding

    Product text helps small catalogs, not big ones

    The "scent" fingerprints are not uniformly useful — and pinning down when they help is one of the paper's main findings.

    🏘️ Small-town library

    Musical Instruments, Office Products: sparse borrowing records, so the librarian leans on the blurbs. Text clearly helps — especially for obscure items almost nobody has bought.

    🏙️ Big-city library

    Video Games, Beauty & PC: so much borrowing history that blurbs are redundant. The same text machinery adds roughly nothing on the same measurements.

    And we didn't stop at correlation: we thinned dense catalogs on purpose — retraining on artificially sparsified data while grading on the same exam — to test whether scarcity itself flips text from useless to useful. Practical upshot: whether to wire product text into your recommender depends on your catalog's density. Measure it first.

    Section 5 · the machinery

    Why trust the numbers: three interlocking mechanisms

    This field's quiet failure mode: tiny improvements, many knobs, and every incentive to report your best run. Most of our paper's machinery exists to make that structurally impossible for us.

    5.1 Pre-declaration = calling your shot

    Before any experiment that could become a claim, we commit a sealed contract to version control: the exact command, settings, fresh never-run random seeds, the pass/fail rule, and the exact sentence we'd be allowed to claim. Like calling the pocket before the pool shot. Misses get published too — the contract is already public, so there's no quiet way to bury one.

    5.2 The fail-closed gate = a printer that refuses to bluff

    All 168 numbers printed in the paper are wired to the raw files they came from. On every change, a build script recomputes them all and refuses to build the paper if one digit disagrees, one origin is untraceable, or one required evidence family is missing. A manifest pins 153 files by cryptographic hash so evidence can't drift afterward. And each counted result has its own referee inside that build: the Office V3 and filter-breadth adjudicators re-run every time, and the build fails unless both say PASS — a counted claim can never silently outlive its evidence.

    5.3 A rival referee, every hour

    A different AI system (Codex) re-audits the project hourly — re-running gates, hunting contradictions, fact-checking citations against the live web — and appends complaints to a public file. Every complaint gets a written, versioned answer. Dozens of rounds have run; real defects were caught, fixed, and remain on the record.

    Section 6 · the honesty centerpiece

    The Office Products story: the machinery catching us

    • Act 1 · sealed attempt
      A second-category pre-declaration (V1)

      Musical Instruments had passed cleanly. We sealed a contract for Office Products; results came in comfortably above the published comparator. So far, a win.

    • Act 1 · the tripwire fires
      VOID — our own referee disallows the goal

      The contract included a comparability check: our simple-baseline score should roughly match theirs on the same data. Ours landed +44% higher — the field was tilted; everything scored high in our environment, so beating their number proved nothing. Under the contract as written: VOID. It still says VOID in the paper, and always will.

    • Act 2 · level the field
      Run their code on our field

      We took the comparator's own published code and ran it, unmodified, in our environment (with disclosed compatibility adapters — a foreign plug in our socket). Their method scored 0.0279 here vs their published 0.0271 — confirming our environment is generous, and explaining the tilt. Labeled honestly as an environment-caveated single run.

    • Act 3 · sealed rematch
      V3 — the goal that counts

      A new sealed contract with the fix built in: clear the bar of their-code-on-our-field (0.0279), not just the published number, on fresh never-touched seeds. Result: 10 out of 10 runs cleared both bars; worst-case plausible averages 0.03033 (K=16) and 0.03024 (K=8). That pass counts. The V1 VOID stands beside it, forever.

    Appendix

    FAQ

    Why are the scores so small (0.03–0.07)? Is the model bad?

    No — it's the open exam. Ranking the one true next purchase against an entire catalog of tens of thousands means even excellent models place it in the top-10 only part of the time. Multiple-choice-style evaluations produce much bigger numbers on the same models; they're just an easier test. Within this exam, third-decimal gaps separate the leaders — like tenths of a second in a sprint.

    Why five seeds? What's a seed anyway?

    Training shuffles data and initializes weights randomly; the seed is the shuffle of that deck. One run can be lucky. Five runs give an average and a spread — and our pass rules use the 95% confidence lower bound: "even our unluckiest plausible average still clears the bar."

    Couldn't you just quietly re-run experiments until they pass?

    That's exactly what pre-declaration blocks. The contract — command, seeds, pass rule, claim sentence — is committed publicly before the run, with seeds that have never been used. A failed contract can't be un-published; the Office V1 VOID is the standing proof that we honor misses.

    What does "the paper refuses to build" actually mean?

    The PDF is produced by a script that first recomputes all 168 printed numbers from raw result files. Any mismatch, any number whose origin can't be traced, any missing evidence family — the script exits with an error and no PDF is produced. Producing the paper is the proof that every number checks out.

    Is the FIR filter just a moving average? Why is that publishable?

    Mechanically it's close — that's its charm. The contribution is showing that a leak-free, backward-only, starts-at-off version of this fifty-year-old idea reliably improves a modern attention-based recommender across four datasets with zero per-category tuning, inside an evaluation rig where the gain can't be an artifact of cherry-picking. Cheap, safe, repeatable beats big and unverifiable.

    Why did you run the competitor's code yourselves?

    Because published numbers come from their hardware and software; ours differ, and our V1 tripwire proved the difference matters (+44% on a baseline). Running their unmodified code in our environment gives an apples-to-apples bar on our field — clearly labeled as a single, environment-caveated run, which is why the sealed V3 contract requires clearing the published number too.

    Who audits the auditors?

    Everything is in one public repository: the sealed contracts, raw result files with cryptographic hashes, the gate script, the rival AI's complaint log, and our signed responses. Anyone can re-run the single verification command and rebuild all 168 numbers from evidence. Trust ends in a command you can run, not in us.
