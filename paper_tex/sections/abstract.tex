We evaluate a small, detachable causal finite-impulse-response (FIR) residual for sequential recommendation on Amazon Reviews 2023 (5-core, full-catalog, leave-last-out). Matched-initialization internal estimates are positive on Musical\_Instruments (+0.002265, ordinary Welch 95\% CI [0.001928, 0.002602]) and, under one untuned configuration, on Industrial\_and\_Scientific (+0.002110 [0.001820, 0.002399]) and CDs\_and\_Vinyl (+0.006150 [0.005849, 0.006450]); the latter two use paired configuration blocks. These are outcome-known or test-exposed filter-versus-identity studies, not independent confirmation. A pre-declared but likewise outcome-known six-arm control study sharpens the mechanism boundary: every temporally active arm improves identity after Holm adjustment, learned taps beat two algebraically redundant fixed-filter parameterizations, but learned-specific advantages over a shared-filter control (-0.000081 [-0.000337, 0.000175]) and a parameter-matched nonlinear causal control (+0.000204 [-0.000038, 0.000446]) are not established. Because there is no active lag-0 or pointwise parameter-matched non-temporal placebo, these results do not isolate temporal structure from generic trainable-residual capacity. Secondary findings are two environment-caveated per-category point-estimate threshold comparisons and a Musical\_Instruments frequency-5-heavy text-tail effect; neither licenses a general system-ranking or cross-dataset claim.
